What drives moves here — and the traps

Dated catalyst

FDA decision, results, investor day, reverse split. Known date, unknown direction. See the track record below for how recent ones played out.

Low float after a split

A 1-for-30 split can leave ~1M shares outstanding. News moves that violently — and a shelf takedown usually follows.

Crowded short

High short interest on a small float means good news forces covering. Short data is twice-monthly and lags.

Reading the meter

Fuel (6 pts): short interest, days to cover, float. Spark (4 pts): shorts losing money on rising volume, short-sale volume, a dated catalyst — all scaled down when few shares are short. Brakes: dilution filings and collapse signs subtract. The dot is confidence: filled = all inputs fresh, half = some missing or stale, empty = mostly guesswork.

The traps

ATMs, equity lines, resale registrations, warrants and resetting converts. Red badges link to the actual EDGAR filings (S-1, S-3, F-1, 424B, EFFECT).

If you trade any of these

  • Limit orders only. Sub-$1 spreads run 5–10%, and sub-cent names trade in fractions of a cent.
  • Size every position as a full loss you can accept — a common rule is 1–2% of the portfolio per name.
  • Open the filing badges first. A fresh 424B means shares are being sold now; an S-1/F-1 resale registration means holders can sell soon; EFFECT means a registration just went live.
  • Expect a trading pause on reverse-split effective dates.
